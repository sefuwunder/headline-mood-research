The through-line

Old machinery, new context

The durable ideas are graded intensity, separate emotional poles, and explicit rules. Recent work adds domain induction, concept-level coverage, continuous affect dimensions, and transformer hybrids.

Lexicons have not disappeared. They have become priors, features, filters, and interpretable checkpoints inside larger systems.
Supported by SentiLARE, SKEP, lexicon-filtered BERT, and comparative “revival” evidence.
1966–2014

Count → weight → shift

General Inquirer and LIWC establish categories; AFINN and SentiStrength add intensity; SO-CAL and VADER make context rules operational.

2016–2024

Induce → adapt → hybridize

SentProp learns domain lexicons from embeddings; newer systems inject lexical knowledge into pre-training or filter neural inputs.

HEADLINE FIT

Emotion, not just polarity

DepecheMood is news-derived; NRC VAD provides continuous affect; SemEval Affective Text offers direct headline validation.

Novel uses

Six patterns worth stealing

These are not just newer papers. They are reusable research moves that change how a lexical system is built, scoped, or evaluated.

01 · INDUCE

Grow a domain lexicon from a small seed set

Build a graph over headline embeddings and propagate labels from carefully chosen fear, hope, pessimism, and optimism seeds.

EvidenceSentProp; DepecheMood++; automatic domain adaptation.

02 · LOCATE

Model emotion as a region in VAD space

Fear is not merely “negative,” and hope is not simply “positive.” Continuous valence, arousal, and dominance can represent their different profiles.

EvidenceNRC VAD; Warriner norms; EmoBank.

03 · PHRASE

Score concepts, not only tokens

Headline meaning often sits in compact phrases—“lose job,” “peace deal,” “risk eases”—whose effect is not the sum of isolated words.

EvidenceSenticNet’s concept-level design.

04 · SCOPE

Replace negation windows with syntactic scope

Use dependency subtrees or a learned scope detector when available; otherwise treat a fixed word window as an explicit approximation.

EvidenceNegBERT, with MPQA, SO-CAL, and VADER as rule-based predecessors.

05 · HYBRID

Use lexical knowledge as a prior or filter

A lexicon can focus a transformer, add interpretable features, or create pre-training objectives rather than compete with the model.

EvidenceSentiLARE; SKEP; lexicon-filtered BERT.

06 · CURATE

Let models propose; let people adjudicate

LLMs can expand colloquial and low-coverage candidates quickly, but human verification remains the quality gate.

EvidenceHuman–LLM collaborative Cantonese emotion lexicon.

Recommended protocol

From word list to defensible scorer

A compact implementation path for the existing headline widget, ordered by expected value before model complexity.

Grade every term

Move from binary membership to signed intensity, for example −5…+5. Preserve source, confidence, and domain notes per entry.

AFINN · SentiStrength

Keep each pole separate

Accumulate fear and hope independently, and pessimism and optimism independently, before deriving a displayed index. Mixed headlines stay visible.

SentiStrength · VADER

Add a small shifter engine

Handle negation, intensifiers, diminishers, contrastive clauses, and irrealis markers. Prefer syntactic scope when available.

VADER · SO-CAL · NegBERT

Expand in-domain, then review

Seed headline embeddings with 20–40 terms per pole, propagate candidate scores, and run a human review pass—optionally with LLM suggestions.

SentProp · DepecheMood++ · Zhang et al. 2024

Add phrase and VAD layers

Match recurring multiword concepts first, then use continuous valence–arousal–dominance norms as a back-off for uncovered words.

SenticNet · NRC VAD · Warriner

Validate where it will be used

Benchmark on SemEval headlines, then hand-label a sample of current HN and Guardian headlines. Audit systematic misses and domain flips.
